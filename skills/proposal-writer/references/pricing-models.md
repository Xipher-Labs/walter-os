# Pricing Models for B2B Engagements

## Fixed-Price

**What it is**: A single agreed price for a defined scope of work.

**When to use**:
- Scope is well-defined and unlikely to change.
- Client needs budget certainty.
- You have done similar work before and can estimate confidently.

**Risk profile**: Risk on the provider. If scope expands or takes longer,
you absorb the cost. Mitigate with a tight SOW and a change management process.

**Example rate card structure**:

```
Phase 1: Discovery and audit — $4,500
Phase 2: Design and architecture — $6,000
Phase 3: Implementation — $12,000
Phase 4: Testing and handoff — $3,500
Total: $26,000
Payment: 50% on signing, 25% at Phase 2 completion, 25% on final delivery.
```

---

## Time and Materials (T&M)

**What it is**: Client pays for actual hours worked at an agreed hourly or
daily rate.

**When to use**:
- Scope is uncertain or likely to evolve.
- Client wants flexibility.
- Research or exploratory work where output is unpredictable.

**Risk profile**: Risk on the client (cost can exceed estimate). Mitigate with
a budget cap or weekly hour reporting.

**Example rate card structure**:

```
Principal consultant: $175/hour
Senior associate: $120/hour
Estimated hours: 80-120 hours
Budget cap: $21,000 (client authorizes additional hours above this in writing)
Invoiced: Bi-weekly, net 15.
```

---

## Retainer

**What it is**: Client pays a fixed monthly fee for an agreed number of hours
or a defined set of ongoing services.

**When to use**:
- Ongoing advisory, fractional executive, or managed service relationship.
- Client needs predictable access to your expertise month-to-month.
- You want recurring revenue.

**Risk profile**: Balanced. Provider commits capacity; client commits budget.
Unused hours typically do not roll over (agree this upfront).

**Example rate card structure**:

```
Monthly retainer: $5,000/month
Includes: Up to 20 hours of advisory and implementation support.
Overage: $225/hour above 20 hours (pre-approved by client).
Term: 3-month minimum, then month-to-month with 30 days notice.
```

---

## Value-Based Pricing

**What it is**: Price is set as a percentage or fixed fee tied to the value
delivered to the client, not hours worked.

**When to use**:
- You can quantify the outcome (e.g., revenue uplift, cost reduction).
- You have strong confidence in the outcome.
- Client is sophisticated and understands the model.

**Risk profile**: High upside for provider; client pays for outcomes. Requires
trust and clear measurement criteria agreed upfront.

**Example structure**:

```
Base fee: $8,000 (covers work regardless of outcome)
Performance fee: 15% of documented cost savings in the first 12 months,
  capped at $30,000.
Measurement: Agreed baseline set at project kickoff; client provides quarterly
  reporting.
```

---

## Milestone-Based

**What it is**: Payment is tied to reaching defined milestones rather than
calendar dates or hours.

**When to use**:
- Long projects where the client wants payments linked to progress.
- You want to reduce payment delay risk.
- Client is risk-averse about paying upfront.

**Example structure**:

```
On contract signing: $5,000
On delivery of prototype: $10,000
On client acceptance of final product: $10,000
30 days after go-live (retention): $5,000
Total: $30,000
```

---

## Choosing a Model — Decision Matrix

| Scenario | Recommended model |
|---|---|
| Well-defined scope, experienced team | Fixed-price |
| Exploratory or research work | T&M |
| Ongoing advisory or fractional role | Retainer |
| Measurable outcome, strong track record | Value-based |
| Long project, client wants payment gates | Milestone-based |
| High-risk scope for both sides | Hybrid: fixed base + T&M overage |
