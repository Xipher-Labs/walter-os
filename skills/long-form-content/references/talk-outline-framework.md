# Conference Talk Outline Framework

## Three-Act Structure

Every effective conference talk follows a narrative arc. The specifics vary by
topic, but the structure is:

**Act 1 — Hook and Problem Frame (20-25% of time)**
- Open with a hook: a story, a provocative statement, or a demonstration.
  The hook must make the audience lean forward within 60 seconds.
- Establish the problem: what is broken, hard, or misunderstood?
- Stake the ground: what will the audience know or be able to do after this talk?
  State this explicitly — audiences that know what they are getting stay engaged.

**Act 2 — Solution, Evidence, and Stories (60-65% of time)**
- Present the core insight or solution in 1-3 key points.
- For each point: lead with the claim, support with evidence or a story, close
  with the implication for the audience.
- Include at least one memorable story — the brain stores stories better than
  abstractions.
- If you have a demo or live example, place it here.

**Act 3 — Synthesis and Call-to-Action (10-15% of time)**
- Synthesize: bring the key points together into a single conclusion.
- Call-to-action: what should the audience do in the next 24 hours? Make it
  specific and achievable (one action, not five).
- Closing: return to the hook if possible (bookending). End on a memorable line.

## Slide Count Guidance

| Duration | Recommended slides | Pace |
|---|---|---|
| 15 minutes | 8-12 slides | ~1.5 min/slide |
| 30 minutes | 15-20 slides | ~1.5 min/slide |
| 45 minutes | 20-30 slides | ~1.5 min/slide |

**Rules**:
- One idea per slide. If a slide needs two sub-bullets, consider splitting.
- Text on slides should be legible from the back row (minimum 24pt font).
- Slides are speaker notes for the audience, not for the speaker. The speaker's
  own notes are in the speaker notes pane.
- Avoid bullet lists — use a single statement per slide, with a visual if possible.

## Slide Outline Template

```
Slide 1: Title slide — talk title, speaker name, conference/date
Slide 2: Hook — [one image or one provocative statement; no bullets]
Slide 3: Problem statement — [one sentence]
Slide 4: Stakes — [who cares and why right now]
Slide 5: "By the end of this talk, you will..." — [one promise]
Slide 6-7: Act 1 wrap / context
Slide 8-N: Act 2 — Key points (3-5 slides per point, including evidence and story)
Slide N+1: Synthesis — [one-sentence conclusion]
Slide N+2: Call-to-action — [one specific action]
Slide N+3: Thank you + Q&A + contact
```

## Speaker Notes Format

For each slide, speaker notes should include:

1. **Transition in**: how you will get to this slide from the previous one
   (one sentence).
2. **Core message**: what you want the audience to remember from this slide
   (one sentence — if you can only say one thing, what is it?).
3. **Talking points**: 3-5 bullet points of what you plan to say (not a
   script — cues only).
4. **Time check**: how long this slide should take.
5. **Transition out**: how you will move to the next slide.

---

## Q&A Preparation

Prepare for 5-10 anticipated questions. For each:

1. Write the question as the audience would ask it.
2. Write a 2-3 sentence answer.
3. If you do not know the answer, prepare a "I don't know, but here is how
   I would think about it" response.

**Hostile question handling**: If someone challenges your premise, use the
"acknowledge, bridge, redirect" pattern:
- Acknowledge what is valid in their challenge.
- Bridge: "And what I've found is..."
- Redirect to your core message.

Never argue with a hostile questioner. Move on after one exchange.

## CFP Abstract Template

```
Title: [Talk title — should reflect the key insight, not just the topic]

Abstract (250-500 words):

[Hook: one sentence that creates curiosity]

[Problem: 2-3 sentences on what is broken or misunderstood]

[Solution preview: 2-3 sentences on what your talk addresses, without giving
everything away]

[Evidence of credibility: one sentence on why you are the right person to
give this talk — experience, research, or unique position]

[Takeaways: 3 bullet points on what attendees will leave with]

[Closing: one sentence reiterating the value to this conference's audience]
```

**CFP tips**:
- Read the conference's past talks to calibrate sophistication level.
- Avoid jargon that the committee might not know.
- Put the most important sentence first — reviewers read dozens of proposals.
- "Practical" and "actionable" are high-value words for most tech conference CFPs.
- If the conference allows it, attach a short speaker bio and any video of
  you speaking (even a low-quality clip helps).
