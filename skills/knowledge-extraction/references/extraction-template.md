## Frameworks and Mental Models

### System 1 / System 2

Two modes of thinking: System 1 is automatic, fast, and emotional — it handles
most of our moment-to-moment decisions without conscious effort. System 2 is
slow, deliberate, and logical — it engages when we face novel or complex
problems. The key insight is that System 1 is running the show most of the time,
and System 2 is prone to fatigue (ego depletion).

### Reference Class Forecasting

To counteract the planning fallacy, find a reference class of similar projects
and ask "how long did those actually take?" rather than reasoning from the
inside view (what you plan to do). The outside view almost always predicts
longer timelines and higher costs.

---

## Actionable Takeaways

1. Before any important decision, slow down and explicitly invoke System 2:
   write out the decision rather than deciding in a meeting.
2. When estimating project timelines, find 3-5 similar past projects and use
   their actual duration, not your optimistic forecast.
3. When you feel certain about something, ask: "what would make me wrong?"
   Uncertainty is a feature, not a bug.

## Open Questions

1. How do you design organizational processes that reduce reliance on System 1
   for high-stakes decisions?
2. Is overconfidence culturally variable, or is it universal?
