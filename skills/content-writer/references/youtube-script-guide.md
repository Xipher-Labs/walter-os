# YouTube Script Guide

Structural reference for the `content-writer` YouTube script mode.

## Intro Hook (First 8 Seconds)

The first 8 seconds determine whether viewers stay or click away.
At 150 words per minute (normal speaking pace), 8 seconds = ~20 words.

Hook formulas:
- **Bold promise**: "In the next 12 minutes, you'll have a pricing model
  you can test tomorrow."
- **Problem statement**: "If you've ever stared at a blank pricing page,
  this video is for you."
- **Counterintuitive open**: "The reason your dev tool is underpriced has
  nothing to do with your features."

**Anti-patterns:**
- "Hey guys, welcome back to my channel." — wastes 3 of your 8 seconds.
- Long channel intros before the content starts.
- Slow topic introduction: get to the promise immediately.

## 3-Act Body Structure

```
[VISUAL: Title card or B-roll establishing context]

## Act 1 — Setup (25% of runtime)
- Establish the problem or situation
- Show why it matters to the viewer
- Preview what they will learn

[VISUAL: diagram, screenshot, or screen recording if applicable]

## Act 2 — Conflict / Content (60% of runtime)
- Core content delivery
- Break into 2–4 sub-sections, each introduced with a verbal marker:
  "First...", "Second...", "Now here's the key part..."
- Use concrete examples after every abstract point
- Callback to the setup problem periodically

[VISUAL: graphics for abstract concepts; code/demo for technical content]

## Act 3 — Resolution (15% of runtime)
- Synthesize the key takeaway in 1–2 sentences
- Answer: "So what do I do with this?"
- Bridge to the outro CTA
```

## Outro CTA

One action only. Choose the most important:
- Subscribe (for growth)
- Watch next video (for depth)
- Click link in description (for conversion)
- Comment with answer to a question (for engagement)

Example outro:
> If you want to run the repricing experiment I described, I've put the
> template in the description below — it's free. And if you found this
> useful, subscribing helps me know what to make more of.
> Next week I'm covering [topic]. See you then.

## Visual Cue Annotations

Use `[VISUAL: description]` markers in the script where editors should
add b-roll, graphics, or screen recordings. Examples:

- `[VISUAL: split-screen of two pricing pages]`
- `[VISUAL: animate the 4 Van Westendorp questions appearing one by one]`
- `[VISUAL: screen recording of the spreadsheet being filled in]`
- `[VISUAL: cut to talking head for emphasis]`

## Pacing

- Speaking pace: 130–150 words per minute for clarity.
- 10-minute video: approximately 1,300–1,500 words of script.
- Add natural pause markers: `[pause]` after key points.
- Vary sentence length: short sentences for emphasis, longer for explanation.
