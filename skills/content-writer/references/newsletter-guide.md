# Newsletter Guide

Structural reference for the `content-writer` newsletter mode.

## Subject Line

The subject line determines open rate. Write two options (A/B test).

Subject line formulas:
- **Curiosity**: "The metric most EMs ignore (and why it matters)"
- **Direct benefit**: "Your Friday digest, automated"
- **Question**: "How long does your weekly engineering update take?"
- **Number**: "3 things I learned from 50 customer interviews"
- **Personal**: "I made a pricing mistake. Here's what I'd do differently."

**Anti-patterns:**
- All-caps words: "IMPORTANT: read this now" — spam filter magnet.
- Misleading urgency: "Limited time" when there's no deadline.
- Vague subject: "This week's update" — no reason to open.
- Long subjects: keep under 50 characters for mobile display.

## Preview Text

The preview text appears next to the subject line in most inboxes.
90 character maximum. Should complement (not repeat) the subject line.

Example:
- Subject: "The metric most EMs ignore"
- Preview: "And why fixing it cut one team's Friday prep time from 4h to 20min"

## Body Structure (Single Story)

Newsletters with one coherent story outperform issue-style roundups for
founder/developer audiences. Structure:

```
[Opening hook — 1-2 sentences that pull the reader in]

[Context — 2-3 sentences establishing why this matters now]

[Core insight — the main thing you're sharing]
  [Supporting detail 1]
  [Supporting detail 2]
  [Example or data point]

[Implication — what the reader should do with this]

[CTA — one action, clearly stated]
```

## CTA Block

One action. Not three. Examples:
- "Try the template I described: [link]"
- "Reply to this email with your biggest pricing challenge."
- "Forward this to a founder dealing with early sales."

**Anti-patterns:**
- "Follow us on Twitter, LinkedIn, and Instagram, plus share this email,
  and check out our blog, and sign up for our product..." — dilutes action.
- No CTA at all: every email should have a clear next step.
- CTA buried at the bottom after 700 words: readers who skim won't see it.

## Word Count

300–700 words. Founder newsletters that consistently perform well stay
under 600 words. Longer reads belong on the blog.

## Formatting

- Short paragraphs: 2–4 sentences maximum.
- Subheadings for sections longer than 200 words.
- Bold key phrases, not random words.
- No more than one image per email (loading time, spam score).
- Plain text fallback: every email should read well without images.
