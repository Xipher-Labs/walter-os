# Mom Test Rules Reference

Source: general framework based on "The Mom Test" by Rob Fitzpatrick (Createspace, 2013).
These are widely taught principles in customer discovery; descriptions below are
original summaries, not reproduced text.

## The six core rules

### Rule 1: Talk about their life, not your idea

Ask about what they already do and experience. Do not pitch your solution.
The moment you describe your product, you invite politeness bias.

- **Bad:** "What do you think of our idea for an automated metrics dashboard?"
- **Good:** "How do you currently prepare your weekly engineering update?"

### Rule 2: Ask about the past, not the future

Future behavior is notoriously unreliable as a signal. Past behavior is evidence.

- **Bad:** "Would you use a tool that did X automatically?"
- **Good:** "Tell me about the last time you had to do X. What happened?"

### Rule 3: Talk about specifics, not generalities

Generalities ("usually," "sometimes," "often") hide the real frequency and severity.
Specific incidents reveal the actual problem.

- **Bad:** "How often do deployments cause issues?"
- **Good:** "Walk me through the last incident you had. What triggered it?"

### Rule 4: Listen for motivations, not features

Customers describe symptoms and workarounds, not solutions. Your job is to
identify the underlying motivation (the JTBD), not to collect feature requests.

- **Bad:** (taking a feature request at face value)
- **Good:** "Why is that important to you? What would you do if you could do that?"

### Rule 5: Ask about money and time spent today

Current spend is a proxy for priority. If they are spending nothing (time or money)
on a problem, it may not be a real priority regardless of what they say.

- **Bad:** "How much would you pay for a solution?"
- **Good:** "What do you currently spend (time or money) on this?"

### Rule 6: Ask who else is involved in the decision

In B2B, the person you interview is rarely the sole buyer. Uncovering the decision
process early prevents wasted sales cycles.

- **Bad:** (assuming the interviewee decides alone)
- **Good:** "If you found a solution you liked, what would the evaluation process look like?"
